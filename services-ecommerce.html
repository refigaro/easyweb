Why most small business e-commerce stores underperform

The most common problem we see isn't the design — it's the buying journey. Customers can't find what they're looking for. The product page doesn't give them enough information to feel confident buying. The checkout has too many steps, or asks for too much before they've committed. Each one of those friction points is a sale that didn't happen.

The second most common problem is speed. A store that loads slowly on mobile loses customers before they even see your products. And since Google ranks slow sites lower, you're also paying the price in search visibility.

We've seen it firsthand. When we rebuilt Frikko HVAC's e-commerce store — fixing the catalog structure, simplifying navigation, and optimizing the checkout — checkout initiations increased 70.2% year-over-year. The products didn't change. The price didn't change. The buying experience did.

Common concerns

I'm on Shopify — can you migrate me to WooCommerce?
Yes. Shopify-to-WooCommerce migrations include product data, customer data, and order history transfer. We scope migrations separately — the price depends on catalog size and complexity. Contact us for a custom quote. Most migrations for small stores (under 500 products) fall in the $1,500–$2,500 range.
What if I have more than 20 products?
We load the first 20 as part of the plan. Additional products can be loaded at $3 each (with images, descriptions, and attributes), or you can upload them yourself through the admin panel after launch using a CSV import — we'll show you how during the training session.
Do I need technical knowledge to manage the store after launch?
No. WooCommerce's admin panel is designed to be manageable by non-technical users. Adding products, processing orders, running a discount, updating a description — all of that is done through a visual interface. The training session covers exactly what you'll need day-to-day.
Why WooCommerce and not Shopify?
WooCommerce runs on WordPress, which means you own the platform, the data, and the store — no monthly platform fees, no transaction fees on top of payment processing, and no limits on customization. Shopify is a good hosted solution for stores that want simplicity at the cost of ownership. WooCommerce is better for stores that want control and lower long-term costs. If Shopify is genuinely the better fit for your situation, we'll tell you.
