The business problem: customers couldn't figure out what to buy

Frikko sells air conditioning equipment — a category where the technical specs matter. BTU capacity, SEER ratings, compatible refrigerants, coverage area. A buyer who picks the wrong unit will have a bad experience and return it. A buyer who can't figure out which unit is right for them won't buy at all.

Their catalog had the information — buried in the wrong places. Product details lived in the frontend as static text, not in a structured backend. That made it nearly impossible to display specs consistently across hundreds of products. Comparison tables were broken or nonexistent. Filtering was limited. And the overall experience was overwhelming enough that customers were bouncing before they ever reached checkout.

A slow, confusing product page isn't a UX problem — it's a revenue problem. Every abandoned session is a sale that didn't happen. Our job was to fix the buying journey from the inside out.

The decisions we made — and why

Three problems, three decisions. Each one had a direct line to the numbers.

1

1 Reorganize the catalog so customers can self-select

We started by auditing competitor sites in the HVAC and home appliance sector. The consistent failure we found: everyone was presenting technical data in a format made for engineers, not for homeowners trying to cool their living room.

We wireframed a new catalog logic — one that led with the questions the buyer was actually asking ("How big is my space?", "What's my budget?") rather than leading with model numbers. Advanced filtering, clear hierarchy, and a simplified purchase path from category to product to cart.

Before writing a line of code, we built a high-fidelity design system in Figma: design tokens, reusable components, and prototyped flows for every product type. That system is what made the next step possible.

2

2 Move all product data to the backend — the real fix nobody warned us about

Here's what I didn't expect when I started: the entire product catalog was managed in the frontend. Every spec table, every downloadable PDF, every comparison chart — hardcoded as static content. That meant you couldn't build a single product template that worked across all products. Every page was a custom job.

The redesign couldn't happen without solving this first. We migrated the entire data structure to the backend using ACF Pro — custom fields for every product type, dynamic comparison tables, automated spec sheets, video galleries, and downloadable technical PDFs. One product template. Hundreds of products. All consistent. 

<?php
  // Single template — works for every product
  $specs = get_field('technical_data');
  $btu   = $specs['btu_capacity']  ?? null;
  $seer  = $specs['seer_rating']   ?? null;

  if($btu): ?>
    <div class="spec-badge">BTU: <?php echo esc_html($btu); ?></div>
  <?php endif; ?>
  if($seer): ?>
    <div class="spec-badge">SEER: <?php echo esc_html($seer); ?></div>
  <?php endif; ?>

ACF Pro lets the product template show or hide specs dynamically — no manual editing per product.

3

3 Optimize for speed without sacrificing functionality

A well-designed product page that loads in 5 seconds is a lost sale on mobile. We built custom CSS components instead of relying on Elementor's built-in elements — this avoided loading unnecessary scripts and cut DOM bloat significantly. Fluid typography with CSS clamp() handled all responsive behavior without media query overload.

All images were converted to WebP, lazy-loaded below the fold, and served at appropriate sizes per device. Caching and script loading were configured to maximize PageSpeed Insights scores on both mobile and desktop.

The result: when Frikko's marketing team later ran a rebrand in 2026 — new visual identity, new design tokens — the architecture held. They updated the tokens, and the changes propagated across the entire site without touching individual pages or breaking any performance metrics.

What the numbers actually tell you

A +70% increase in checkout initiations doesn't come from making a site look prettier. It comes from removing the reasons people don't check out. In Frikko's case, those reasons were: couldn't find the right product, couldn't understand the specs, couldn't trust the information was accurate.

The +97.8% increase in average engagement time tells you people were actually reading — exploring product specs, comparing options, making decisions. That's what a well-structured catalog does: it converts confusion into confidence.

And because the site launched in June — right at the start of peak HVAC season — those improvements hit at the highest-traffic moment of the year. Timing is strategy.
