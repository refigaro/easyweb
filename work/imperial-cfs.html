The business problem: a $0 self-service tool that nobody was using

Imperial CFS handles thousands of shipping containers moving through the Port of Los Angeles every month. Their clients — importers, freight forwarders, customs brokers — need to do three things constantly: check if their cargo has arrived and cleared customs, see outstanding storage fees, and pay those fees to release their shipment.

The portal to do this existed. But the interface was a table-heavy legacy system that overwhelming users with raw data and no clear hierarchy. Rather than fight with it, clients picked up the phone and called customer service instead. The operational cost of that workaround — staff time, wait times, error rates — was the real problem we were hired to solve.

The brief was direct: redesign the portal so clients can track cargo and process payments themselves, without calling anyone. Every design decision was measured against that goal.

Complex tasks completed in 4 clicks or less

The redesigned availability dashboard handles cargo lookup, status review (Paid / Pending / Holds), and payment initiation — all without leaving the page or calling support.

How we approached it

The work started with research, not wireframes. Skipping that step is why most portal redesigns fail.

1

User research and pain point mapping

Using AI-assisted analysis alongside direct research, we mapped the typical user journeys of freight forwarders and importers interacting with cargo systems. The patterns were consistent: users arrived with a specific task in mind (check one container, pay one invoice), but the interface presented them with unfiltered data tables and forced them to scan through dozens of rows to find what they needed.

We built user personas representing the two primary user types — the importer checking their own cargo and the customs broker managing multiple client shipments. Their workflows and priorities differed significantly, and the original interface served neither well.

2

UX flows and cognitive load reduction

We mapped revised user journeys focused on minimizing steps and eliminating decisions the system should be making for the user. The core principle: a user who arrives knowing their container number should never have to think about what to do next — the interface should surface their specific cargo immediately and show them exactly what action is available.

Status states were a key design decision. "Available", "Pending", "Holds", "Paid" — each needed to be visually distinct and immediately scannable, especially for brokers managing 20+ containers at once. We used color, iconography, and information hierarchy to make status legible at a glance without requiring the user to read each row.

3

Figma prototypes — designed and defended in English

Every design decision was validated through high-fidelity Figma prototypes presented directly to the Imperial CFS team in Los Angeles. Working in English, across time zones, we walked through UX rationale, negotiated priorities when business requirements conflicted with UX best practices, and reached clear implementation agreements before any code was written.

This is the part that most offshore projects skip — the direct, substantive conversation with the client about why specific design choices were made, and what the tradeoffs are. It's also what makes the difference between a project that ships and a project that stalls in revision rounds.

4

Frontend development and handoff

Production-ready code built in HTML, CSS, and Vanilla JavaScript with Bootstrap as the component foundation. The stack was chosen to match the existing backend infrastructure and ensure the development team could integrate the new interface without a framework migration.

The availability dashboard was the most technically complex deliverable — it uses drawers, accordion panels, and contextual checkboxes to present dense logistics data without overwhelming the user. The payment flow was rebuilt as a single-page multi-step experience, eliminating the page reloads that were breaking user context during checkout.

What we delivered

Three interfaces, each solving a specific breakdown in the original user experience.

01

Homepage

Redesigned to surface the three most critical actions immediately: check cargo availability, track ground transportation, and pay storage fees. SEO structure applied from the ground up.

02

Availability Dashboard

The biggest headache. Transformed from a table-heavy legacy interface into a visually scannable tool with clear status states (Paid, Pending, Holds). Complex tasks done in 4 clicks. Drawers, accordions, contextual checkboxes.

03

Payment Flow

Rebuilt as a single-page multi-step experience. Instead of page reloads breaking user context, the entire payment process — invoice review, amount confirmation, payment submission — happens in one flow with guided steps.
