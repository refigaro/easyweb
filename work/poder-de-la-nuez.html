The problem: a campaign without a solid digital landing point loses half its investment

The American Pecan Promotion Board runs a national campaign in Mexico to increase pecan consumption — TV spots, in-store activations at Soriana and HEB, social media content, influencer partnerships. When any of those touchpoints works, the interested consumer goes looking for more information online.

If they land on a slow site, a confusing layout, or a page that doesn't match the campaign's energy — they bounce. The media buy that drove them there is wasted. The activation that caught their attention in the supermarket aisle delivers nothing.

The brief was to build a website that could hold the user's attention long enough to deliver the campaign's message — benefits, recipes, educational content — and convert passive curiosity into genuine engagement with pecans as a food choice.

How I built it

1

Storytelling design — immersion over information dump

Campaign websites fail when they treat the homepage as a brochure. A list of benefits and a logo. This site was designed as a narrative — each section builds on the previous one, pulling the user deeper into the pecan story rather than presenting facts to skim.

Full-screen campaign graphics from the existing visual assets were integrated at key points to create immersion. The visual language mirrors what the user has already seen in the campaign — on TV, in the store, on social — which creates continuity instead of a jarring mismatch between offline and online experience.

2

Blog + recipe CMS — content the client manages independently

The campaign's value lives in its content: recipes, nutritional information, usage ideas. If publishing a new recipe required a developer, the content would slow down and the site would go stale. WordPress with a structured recipe CMS means the client's team publishes new recipes directly — no technical knowledge required, no tickets to file.

Each recipe and blog post was SEO-optimized individually — keyword-targeted titles, meta descriptions, schema markup for recipes (which helps them appear in Google's recipe results). The 18.3% CTR from homepage to content reflects that the content architecture works: users find what they came for.

3

Inclusive design and performance optimization

A national campaign reaches users on cheap Android phones with 3G connections. The site needed to work for all of them — not just users on laptops with fiber internet. Inclusive design principles guided decisions about color contrast, touch target sizes, and font legibility. Images were optimized aggressively to reduce load times without sacrificing the campaign's visual quality.

The result is a site that doesn't require a high-end device to use — which matters when the campaign's goal is mass-market adoption of pecans as a food choice.

Data-driven iteration: the 404 page that changed everything

After launch, analytics revealed a meaningful volume of users hitting broken URLs — and bouncing. The original site had no custom 404 page: broken links sent users to a generic server error, and they left.

I designed and deployed a custom 404 page — on-brand, with navigation shortcuts to the most popular content sections. Within 30 days of the deployment, bounce rate for error traffic dropped to under 2%. One small page, one major improvement to user retention. This is what post-launch iteration actually looks like when you're reading the data.

What the site does

Recetas

Full recipe CMS. SEO-optimized per recipe, with schema markup for Google recipe results. Client-managed — new recipes published without developer intervention.

Blog

Editorial content about pecan culture, health research, and campaign updates. Drives weekly return visits (25.9% stickiness) by giving users a reason to come back.

The campaign was renewed for 2025. A chatbot is currently being integrated into the site. When a campaign performs well enough to get renewed — and the client asks for the website to keep growing — that's the most honest measure of whether the digital platform worked.

What this shows about building for campaigns

The website is where campaigns land — or die

Any campaign that drives people to a URL needs that URL to work. Ads, activations, influencer posts, QR codes — they all end at the website. If the website fails, the entire upstream investment is wasted.

Analytics drive decisions, not assumptions

The 404 improvement wasn't a guess — it came from reading the data after launch and acting on what it showed. This is how good websites improve over time rather than staying static after launch day.

Content independence keeps campaigns alive

A website that requires a developer to publish a new recipe is a website that will go stale. The CMS setup here means the client's team adds content on their own timeline — no bottlenecks, no dependency on technical support for routine updates.

Mass-market campaigns need mass-market performance

A campaign targeting national grocery shoppers reaches people on low-end devices and slow connections. Inclusive design and performance optimization aren't optional for a campaign at this scale — they're the baseline.
