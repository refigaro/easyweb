You've probably seen other agencies with teams "somewhere in Latin America" that are hard to reach, slow to respond, and disappear the week after launch. That's not a geography problem — it's an operations problem. Plenty of bad agencies are in New York too.

Here's what's actually different about Easyweb: I work during your business hours. A full-stack developer in Mexico earns roughly $28,000/year. The same person in the US earns around $147,000. That's the gap that makes a local agency charge you $8,000 for a site we deliver at $1,500. The savings aren't because the quality drops — they're because the overhead doesn't exist.

Easyweb is registered as an LLC in the United States. Your contract is with an American company. Intellectual property transfers to you on delivery, protected under the US-Mexico-Canada Agreement (USMCA). There's legal accountability — the same kind you'd have with any US agency.

The accountability part

The most common concern about working with someone outside the US is: "What happens if something goes wrong?" Here's the short answer.

US-Registered LLC

Easyweb operates as a Limited Liability Company registered in the United States. You're contracting with an American company — not a freelancer with a PayPal account.

USMCA Protection

All intellectual property — code, design, content — transfers to you upon delivery. This is protected under Chapters 19 and 20 of the US-Mexico-Canada Agreement. Enforceable under North American law.

Contracts in English

Every project starts with a signed Master Service Agreement in English — scope, deliverables, payment milestones, IP transfer, and dispute resolution. No ambiguity.

Milestone payments

You don't pay the full amount upfront. Payments are tied to project milestones — design approved, development complete, launch. You keep control of the money until work is delivered.

REPSE compliance: Freelancers and contractors working on your project are engaged under Mexico's REPSE framework (Registro de Prestadoras de Servicios Especializados), which legally governs subcontracting in Mexico. This isn't fine print — it means you're not dealing with an informal operation.

How a project actually works

No surprises. Here's the process from first message to launched site.

1

Free site audit or discovery call (30 min)

We talk about your business, your goals, and what's not working with your current site (or why you need one). I'll tell you honestly if Easyweb is the right fit or not.

2

Proposal + contract

I send a written proposal with scope, timeline, and exact price. No vague estimates. You sign an MSA in English before anything starts.

3

Design — you approve before code starts

5

Launch + handoff

We go live. I walk you through how to manage your own content — or that's what the Monthly Care Plan is for, if you'd rather not deal with it.
