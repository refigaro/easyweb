How Much Does a Small Business Website Cost in 2026? (Honest Breakdown)

The short answer: anywhere from $0 to $15,000+, depending on who builds it and what "built" actually means. The real question isn't what it costs — it's what each option is actually worth to your business.

Before we get into numbers, one thing to establish: there are two very different kinds of cost. What you pay the agency (or DIY tool). And what a bad website costs you in missed customers, lost credibility, and the time you'll spend rebuilding it 18 months later. Most "cheap" options only account for the first.

Here's the breakdown by option.

The 4 Main Options and What They Actually Cost

There isn't a single answer because there isn't a single thing called "a website." Let's break it down by path.

DIY (Wix/Squarespace) $0 $200–$500/yr Template site. Slow. Limited SEO. You do all the work.

DIY Website Builders: The Real Cost

Wix and Squarespace feel free until you need something real. A slow site on a generic template isn't just ugly — it's a business problem. Google penalizes slow sites in rankings. Mobile users abandon sites that take more than 3 seconds to load. And you can't call support when your checkout breaks the night before a product launch.

If you have 20 hours to learn a new tool and your business doesn't depend on web traffic, DIY works. If you're running a real business, it usually doesn't.

Freelancers: When They Work and When They Don't

A good freelancer at $1,500–$3,000 can build an excellent site. The problem is finding one — and keeping them. The most common complaint we hear from new clients: "Our last developer disappeared after launch." No support, no updates, no answers. For a business website that needs ongoing care, this is a structural risk, not a one-off bad experience.

Freelancers work best for short, defined projects where you don't need post-launch support. For everything else, a company with accountability is worth the difference in price.

US Local Agencies: What You're Actually Paying For

The $8,000–$15,000 quote from a local agency isn't always unjustified. You're paying for their office rent, their account manager, their billing department, and the overhead of a local business. The actual code and design often costs the same as anywhere else.

If you need hand-holding through every step and want to drive to their office, that premium makes sense. If you're comfortable with video calls and clear communication, you're overpaying for geography.

Nearshore Agencies: The Option Most Business Owners Don't Know Exists

Nearshore means the team is in a neighboring country (for US businesses, usually Mexico or Latin America), operates in your time zone, communicates in English, and charges based on local labor costs — not US overhead.

The math is simple: a full-stack developer in Mexico earns ~$28,300/year. The same developer in the US costs ~$146,900/year. That's 5x the cost for the same output. Nearshore agencies pass that difference to the client.

The catch: you need to trust them. That's why we're a US-registered LLC. Not a random offshore team — a legal US entity with accountability, USMCA protections, and the same legal standing as any American company you'd hire.

What Affects the Price the Most

  • Number of pages: A 5-page business site costs less than a 20-page e-commerce store.
  • Custom design vs. template: Custom design takes more time. It also converts better.
  • E-commerce functionality: Payment processing, inventory, checkout flows — these add complexity.
  • Content creation: If the agency writes your copy, add 20–30% to any quote.
  • Revisions: Unlimited revisions sounds great until it becomes how you justify a bad process.

What We Recommend Budgeting

For a small business getting its first professional website or redesigning an old one, a realistic and honest budget is $500–$2,000 upfront, plus $100–$200/month for hosting, maintenance, and support.

Below $500, you're in freelancer or DIY territory — and the risk profile changes significantly. Above $2,000, make sure you understand exactly what the extra cost is buying you.
